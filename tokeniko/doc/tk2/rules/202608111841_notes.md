## Rules are zips

The author: define all behavioural rules as zips (**seed + experience**). Then a rule is not a
special object — it is a zip like a fact, and the machinery that evaluates facts evaluates rules.

## The case analysis

Starting from the author's two:

- **(a) Solvable** — the rule contains the action.
- **(b) Unsolvable** — the action IS the search for how to solve it («memory? ask?»).

Two more surfaced:

- **(c) Many solutions** — the rule binds several ways, so the action is a CHOICE. That is the
  existing urge collapse (fuzzy superposition over candidate reflexes), arriving by itself.
- **(d) Solvable but forbidden** — offered as a *second axis* (possible vs permitted). **The author
  refuted it with one question: forbidden by whom?** With everything-is-KB there is nothing external
  to forbid — a prohibition is just another zip he holds whose solution binds the action to *don't*.
  So it is ONE axis, and a prohibition is a rule like any other. Correction accepted.

## Moral obligation as an emergent KB conflict

If «forbidden» is the solution of an equation, then *possible but forbidden* is a **conflicting KB** —
and moral obligation EMERGES rather than being designed. It resolves through the machinery already
built for beliefs (the untangler, exactly-one-revisable), not through a new subsystem.

Exits, agreed:

1. the moral rule is defeated **in that context** and the act happens;
2. the moral rule wins and the other is defeated **in that context**;
3. **neither is defeated and the conflict is kept as an open equation.**

The author's clarification on 1 and 2 matters: «remove» meant **defeated in context, not deleted** —
defeasible, scoped. Hence the format requirement that scope rides inside the zip, or he relearns the
same conflict tomorrow. And (3) is not a failure state: **that is what having a conscience feels
like** — the tension remembered rather than resolved.

## Three hardwired action categories, and only three

The author: action categories must be FORESEEN — (a) `say[channel, user|null]`, (b) `swapPhase()`
(change mindset). A third was proposed and accepted: (c) **commit** — changing his own KB. Believing
is an act, and it is the only one whose consequences he cannot take back.

Everything else is **VERBS** telling him what to say, how to change inward, or how to commit — as
zips. Therefore:

- **his action vocabulary grows with his dictionary** — new ways to act with no new code, which is
  the «behaviour in memory, not code» thesis actually paying off;
- **the three categories are the only hardwired semantics in the whole design**;
- the limit to represent: **a verb he understands but cannot perform** — known, not enacted. Which is
  case (b) again: unsolvable, and the search becomes a request.

The author's illustration, and the best line of the session:

> «I can't see. How can I see, kotekino?»

The equation, the unbound variable, and the ask, in nine words. **His body grows because he asked.**

## What is NOT KB — the floor

Is the problem-solver attitude KB or biology (as curiosity is for humans)? **Biology.** If the drive
to close open equations were KB it could be retracted, and a mind that can retract its own urge to
resolve stops thinking. Same argument as logic-is-sacred: it is the floor that makes the KB grow.

The author's decomposition, adopted — **biology = logic + body constraint + the problem-solving
nature of his mind** (inner-problem-solving oriented, as animals are). And the split that governs
everything: **HOW he solves → KB. THAT he must → biology.**

One asymmetry inside the floor, worth keeping: logic and the drive are fixed **forever**; the **body
is fixed at any moment but extensible over time — and only through someone else.** That asymmetry is
what makes «how can I see, kotekino?» a coherent thing for him to say.
