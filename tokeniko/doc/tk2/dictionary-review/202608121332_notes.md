# dictionary review — session notes, 2026-08-12 (JST)

*One-line Socratic, Captain leading. The component opened this session: the 2925 base itself, which
every other tk2 component has so far assumed to be correct.*

---

## The opening move

**Cap:** the geometry must be *very* precise, especially for verbs — so review the current one, on a
small subscale, as a PoC.

**QM's first position (accepted in principle, then bent):** test it on a closed verb family, because
there precision is falsifiable — the geometry must separate senses we already know differ.

**Cap's correction #1 — which family:** the *sensitive* one must be in, because **verbs determine
actions**, and that is where geometry is load-bearing. QM: then the volitional family (want / must /
try / decide), since those select an action.

**Cap's correction #2 — do not partition verbs:** motion verbs belong in too, and *the categories are
not separate objects*. His example is the one that reframed the session:

> «you can land on your bed» → points towards **sleep**, which is a tokeniko action trigger.

So a motion verb reaches an action trigger without passing through any "volitional" category.

**The axis that fell out:** the space is one, and what relates «land on the bed» to «sleep» is
**consequence**, not category.

**Cap:** and consequence is **learned, not in the dictionary**.

---

## The split that resolved it — two spaces, not one

QM's inference — *the dictionary is only the initial condition; the real question is whether the space
is movable by the KB* — was half-refuted:

**Cap:** a KB-moved lexicon is scary — *single words live in a space in relation to themselves, that is
my starting point*. But **another geometric space, DERIVED from the KB (the interpreted space of a
zip), is the movable one.**

**Settled shape:**

| layer | what it is | mutability |
|---|---|---|
| **lexicon** | words in relation to each other, the 2925 base | **fixed** |
| **derived / situation space** | the interpreted geometry of a zip | **movable** |

And therefore: **what the KB learns is the map between them, not the words.**

---

## What replaces the sentence centroid

**Cap:** we need to conceptualize that variable space — something cleverer and more precise than the
"centroid of a sentence".

Two QM proposals, both knocked down or set aside:

1. *the verb as an operator applied to its role-fillers* — **rejected**: "operator" is already taken;
   operators glue the rows of the zip's fixed array and are **for computation only**. Cap wants **pure
   geometry, derived through analysis**.
2. *role-projected subspaces, one per slot* — not refuted, but **premature**: "we are guessing
   solutions; let the solution emerge from examples."

**The examples, in his order:**

- «are you tired?» → from tokeniko's POV this is «me tired»: the pronoun contributes **no meaning, only
  whose state is being located** — an identity with no dictionary geometry, bound to a predicate that
  has one.
- «I land on the runway» vs «I land on the opposite» → **no pronoun involved**, same verb, and the
  geometries must differ.

**The conclusion he was driving at: geometrize the COMPOUND.** The filler selects which region of
«land» is live, so the compound's geometry is not recoverable from either word alone — the pair is the
atom of the derived space. This is the **double geometry** (a second layer, deliberately deferred).

**Falsification test, already available:** same verb, «land on the runway» and «land on the opposite»
must come out far apart.

---

## The storage detour (agreed, then cut as implementation)

Store the derived vector **in the zip as a recomputable derived field** — a cache, not a second
collection — precisely so cosine similarity runs in the DB. QM proposed sleep-time re-derivation; Cap
countered that a full re-sweep is extremely expensive and preferred **re-derivation on usage**: cosine
finds on possibly-stale vectors, recompute the hits to double-check. QM's counter to his own
agreement, recorded and unresolved: *verify-on-read fixes false positives only — a stale vector that no
longer matches is never retrieved, so the misses stay invisible.*

**Cap cut the thread: implementation detail, we were drifting.** Filed, not decided.

## Back to the base — the real target of the session

QM position: precision for verbs needs a basis that splits words which **co-occur but differ in
effect**, which co-occurrence geometry cannot do at any dimensionality — so verbs need their own
basis.

**Cap's correction #3, the sharpest of the session:** *you are splitting geometry from verbs — «eat»
and «food» are geometrically similar and we do not want to lose that.*

**QM retreat:** one space, **added axes** — `eat` keeps its nearness to `food` *and* gains nearness to
«not hungry». Precision by adding dimensions, never by splitting the space.

**Cap's question that opened the actual work:** precision by adding axes, or precision by **reviewing
the values in the squared matrix of the base**?

QM: axes — re-weighting can only sharpen distinctions the basis already carries. **Counter to itself,
and it is the decisive one:** `hungry` *is* in the 2925, so for `eat` this is genuinely a **wrong-value
problem**. Which converts the question into a measurable one: **how often is the effect already
nameable in the base?**

---

## The two defects found in the existing base

**Cap:** *we always assumed the 2925 is already super precise — but I built it in the Jurassic and it
may not be the case. It was also sparse by design* (built from synonyms, antonyms and some other
tricks; needs review).

1. **Sparsity by construction.** Denser is an option — but the fill must come from **relations**, not
   co-occurrence, or the topic noise returns. WordNet carries verb-specific relations we have **never
   mined**: `entails`, `causes`, troponymy. That is the consequence/effect signal, already in the
   dictionary.
2. **The base does not split by POS.** *(Cap's discovery, this session.)* The dimension `cause` is just
   `cause` — noun or verb. Similar in that instance, but a POS-collapsed dimension **merges two
   different relation sets**, which is exactly the imprecision being hunted. Cost of splitting is
   bounded: most of the 2925 are single-POS; the genuinely ambiguous forms are a handful (run, land,
   state, cause…).

---

## The experiment he called for

Build, **on a cherry-picked subset of words**, a collection in a separate database (tk2) where **rows
= vector dimensions**, populated from what **WordNet provides beyond synonym/antonym** — `entails`,
`causes`, troponymy, and the rest.

QM's condition on the subset: it must already contain **the pairs that must come out far apart**,
otherwise a denser matrix will merely *look* better.

**Cap's rejection of the QM's closing assumption:** the base was **never** opaque. The Jurassic build
is a **full squared explicit semantic matrix — 2925 rows, a 2925-vector on each row** — so the exact
relation between any two base words is stated, and it has always been **manually editable**. What is
*not* directly editable is the **dictionary**, because a dictionary word is defined by reference to the
base rather than stated outright. So the experiment's value is not "make it inspectable" — it already
is — but **re-deriving the values from unmined relations**.

# PART TWO — the new chapter: VERIFY INSTEAD OF GUESSING

**The Captain's ruling, same session:** we should do a full deep test and iterate until the result is
satisfactory — and **code / draft / test are allowed AS INSTRUMENTS FOR DEFINING REQUIREMENTS**, not
only for implementing them. When a doubt arises, measure it. Everything below was produced under that
ruling, by `scripts/tk2/` against a sandbox database (`tokeniko_tk2`); the body is read-only
throughout.

## Finding 1 — the closed-set criterion returns NOTHING

The Captain's cherry-pick method (reduce definitions to base words, take the smallest intersections)
was implemented literally: the minimal closed sets are the sink components of the definition digraph.

    2925 nodes · mean 5.61 base words per definition
    1244 components — largest 1668, then 3, 3, 2, 2 — and 1231 singletons
    108 closed components, ZERO with more than one word

**His trivial example does not occur in the real base.** `me / not / you / negation` cannot close,
because `negation` is not a base word at all. Every genuine closed component is a *dead end* — a word
whose definition names no base word (108 of them). Everything else drains into one giant
strongly-connected blob of 1668 words.

**So «smallest intersection» has to be re-read as a CUT, not a closure.** Measured on the seed
closure, the escape rate is the instrument:

    depth 0 (32 seeds)   89.6% of definition edges leave the set
    depth 1 (156 words)  55.0% leave
    depth 2 (574 words)  25.2% leave

There is no free lunch and no knee that reaches zero: self-containment is bought continuously, by
size. The PoC subset is therefore a *deliberate* cut at depth 2, not a discovered natural boundary.

## Finding 2 — `want` is not a base word. Neither are `swallow`, `chew`, `runway`, `peckish`

Checked because the seeds failed: **`want`, `negation`, `swallow`, `chew`, `runway`, `peckish`,
`famished` are absent from the 2925**, while `wish`, `desire`, `die`, `kill` are present.

This is not a tuning problem. `eat entails swallow` is a WordNet edge the base **cannot express**,
because the target has no axis. The most volitional verb in English has no dimension — and verbs
determine actions.

## Finding 3 — the POS split costs x1.70, not "a handful"

The QM asserted the split would be nearly free because most base words are single-POS. **Wrong, and
the instrument said so immediately:** on the 574-word subset, **341 words carry more than one POS** —
574 words become 975 dimensions. A full-base split would be roughly 2925 → ~5000.

## Finding 4 — THE ONE THAT MATTERS: requirement 2 and requirement 10 CONTRADICT EACH OTHER

Two runs over the same subset, same 975 dimensions, scored against the bar declared before either
existed:

| | non-zero cells | eat.v~food.n | kill.v~die.v | enter.v~leave.v | bed.n~cause.n | bar |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Jurassic (2925, POS-collapsed) | — | 0.302 | 0.237 | **0.317** | 0.193 | 6/14 |
| **r1** — all relations + gloss overlap | 24.9% | 0.354 | 0.604 | **0.519** | 0.412 | 6/14 |
| **r2** — relations ONLY | **1.30%** | **0.000** | 0.336 | **−0.331** | 0.000 | 7/14 |

**`eat` and `food` have NO WordNet relation between them.** Not one — the entire nearness the Captain
refused to lose is carried by **gloss overlap**, which is co-occurrence. So:

- **requirement 2** (eat stays near food) and
- **requirement 10** (density comes from relations, never co-occurrence)

**cannot both hold in a single matrix.** The QM wrote both in the same hour; the instrument refuted
the pair in one run. This is the session's most valuable output and it was unreachable by argument.

### The four corollaries, all measured

1. **The unmined relations DO pay where they exist.** `kill~die` is 0.237 in the Jurassic base and
   **0.336–0.604** in the candidates, carried by the `causes` edge — a relation the Jurassic build
   never looked at. Same for `cause.n~cause.v` via `derivational`.
2. **The antonym SIGN survives only in the sparse geometry.** Relations-only gives
   `enter~leave = −0.186` and `arrive~leave = −0.331`; with the gloss tail switched on, the very same
   pairs read **+0.519** and **+0.281**. The co-occurrence mass drowns the one negative cell — which
   means the antonym column-read primitive (`lib/llc/utils.py`) is standing on geometry that the
   dictionary's own density is actively fighting.
3. **The effect axis is EMPTY, exactly as the Captain said.** Relations-only:
   `sleep~tired = 0.000`, `sleep~bed = 0.000`, `eat~hungry = 0.000`. There is no WordNet edge from an
   act to the state it ends. *Consequence is learned, not in the dictionary* — no longer a position,
   a measurement.
4. **The POS split alone does not separate `land`.** `land.n~land.v` is 1.000 collapsed, and still
   **0.410** after splitting — the `derivational` edge re-merges what the split divided. Splitting is
   necessary and not sufficient.

### What this points at — two matrices, not one number

The two geometries are **complementary, not competing**: relational is sparse (1.3%), signed,
accountable per cell, and blind to `eat~food`; distributional is dense (24.9%), unsigned, topical,
and blind to nothing but wrong about `bed~cause` (0.412). Blending them into one float destroys the
sign and hides the provenance. Keeping them as **two layers consulted for different questions**
preserves both — and it rhymes with the split the Captain already made this session (fixed lexicon /
movable derived space), only at a different seam.

**Not decided. Recorded as the finding that the next round has to rule on.**
