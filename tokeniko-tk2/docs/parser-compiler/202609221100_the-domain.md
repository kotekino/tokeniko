# parser/compiler — THE DOMAIN, THE FIFTH PREFIX ELEMENT, 2026-09-22

*Opened as «fix `aw-15`». It was not a defect.*

**THE STATION HAD NEVER RAISED A `DomainRow`.** `grep DomainRow tk2/language/` was empty — while
the schema had held the element since E2 (*«the context a claim holds in: legally · in chess · as a
doctor · in Italy»*), the decompiler could already say one, the drill hand-compiled three cases, and
rules reqs 6–7 depend on it: «as a doctor I disagree; as a father I understand» must be two
positions honestly held, not a KB contradiction.

One side of the format was built, the other never was, and the whole gap sat under a single red
case on the roadmap.

    drill gate   65 agreed · 5 DISAGREED  ->  66 agreed · 4 DISAGREED   (`aw-15` closed)
    fixpoint     68 of 87  ->  69, and READ WHOLE 70 -> 72
    prefix       29 of 89 paired -> 31; their slots agreeing 69 -> 71

**THE READ-WHOLE JUMP IS THE ONE THAT MATTERS.** `aw-15`'s France was `unplaced` — dropped
entirely, because Italy had taken the single `location` box. The domain does not merely label Italy
correctly; it gives France its box back.

## THE OBVIOUS RULE IS WRONG, AND THE BENCH IS WHY WE KNOW

*«A marked nominal before the subject is a domain»* fits all three drill cases. `tools/domain_bench.py`
measured it at **five false positives out of six** — it swallows «In the morning, I go to work» (a
TIME), «With a knife, he cut the bread» (an INSTRUMENT), «At home, I relax» (a LOCATION). Fronting
is necessary and nowhere near sufficient.

**A FALSE POSITIVE IS THE SIN HERE** (req 8), and worse than usual: a domain nobody stated indexes a
claim to a context it was never held in, and the evaluator would then **never contradict it**. A
miss only leaves the station where it already was. So both signals were chosen for zero false
positives, at the cost of three of the six domains the bench holds.

    rule                                                  caught  missed  FALSE+
    fronted alone                                              6       0       5
    marker is `as`                                             2       4       0
    fronted ADVERB                                             1       5       0
    `as` OR fronted-adverb OR fronted-with-box-contested       5       1       0

## THE TWO SIGNALS BUILT

**A fronted `as`** — «AS A DOCTOR I disagree». The one marker English keeps for the frame itself,
never for a participant. *A single word and not a roster: the bench found no second marker that
separates a frame from a participant, and a second arrives with its own evidence or not at all.*

**A fronted phrase whose BOX IS CONTESTED** — «In Italy, you may drive IN FRANCE». Two phrases
wanting one box is the only evidence in the tree that one of them is not about the event, and the
fronted one yields. That is the drill's own reading: *Italy is the jurisdiction, France is where the
driving happens.*

**THE CONTEST HAS TO BE SEEN BEFORE THE LOOP RUNS.** By the time a sentence-order walk reaches the
second claimant the first has already taken the box, so `_domains` reads the whole clause at once.

*And the bench's first version got this wrong in a way worth recording: it compared UD **deps**, so
«In the morning, I go TO WORK» looked like a clash — two `obl`s — when the morning is a TIME and the
work a DESTINATION. The compiler already works that out by running the marker selector over the
supersense. Asking the compiler what it DID, instead of guessing from the relation, turned a rule
with one false positive into a rule with none.*

## AND ONE CASE WHERE NO RULE CAN EXIST

    « Legally, in Italy, he is still married. »   in Italy  -> DOMAIN
    « In France, I ate well. »                    in France -> LOCATION

**Identical on every fact the parse gives** — same relation, same marker, same fronting, same comma,
neither contested. Nothing in the tree separates them and one of them is a domain. So the station
**abstains** on a bare uncontested fronted locative, and `aw-17` stays red *honestly* rather than
going green on a rule that would invent domains everywhere else.

That is the placement floor's answer again (`202609191500_the-placement-floor.md`, where the honest
result was that no floor exists) and the confidence bench's. **A measurement that says «you cannot
tell» is a result.**

## WHAT IS LEFT, AND WHY EACH IS LEFT

- **A fronted ADVERB** — «Legally,» is a domain and needs `legally` → `law.n`, a derivation the
  compiler has no reader for. *The decompiler already refuses an unmarked domain* («fronting the
  bare noun would say «Law, he is married», which is not the claim»), so compiling one could not
  yet be said back either.
- **A NON-fronted `as`** — «I work as a teacher» was never benched, and `as` there may well be the
  job rather than the frame. Both signals therefore require fronting, which is exactly what the
  evidence covers. *Reading it as a domain would be a claim on no evidence, which is the thing the
  bench exists to prevent.*
