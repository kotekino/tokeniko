## The ladder, from the bottom

A zip is one thought as a **row of fixed boxes**, each holding a vector, so two thoughts compare by
arithmetic instead of by reading. The Captain immediately widened it, correctly: *«tkzip is his own
way to think — it doesn't just hold an input but also all the rules on which tokeniko's mind works,
because everything is knowledge and logic is hardwired.»* A rule is the same boxes twice, glued by
an operator (rules req 1). The format has to hold a fact he heard and a rule he lives by in one
shape; that is the real bar.

**Fixed size means every zip has every box, always — empty ones included.** «The cat sleeps» carries
the four it does not use. That is what makes box 4 comparable to box 4; variable shape would mean
graph matching instead of cosine, and the O(1) thesis dies (req 1, never reopened).

## RULING 1 — the role inventory is FRAME

The question put: *where is the list of boxes written down — code, or db rows?* The QM opened
leaning **db**, by the standing law's own test 2 (*could evidence revise it? then it is knowledge*),
with the dictionary's build/seal discipline as the model.

**The Captain ruled code, and the argument is better than the one it refuted:**

> *«Language structure is common knowledge, well coded and hardly changing in its core. Vocabulary
> changes over time. A direct complement slot or a causal complement — they will remain in English
> until English survives. So we should be good enough to foresee ALL complements / possible slots and
> that's it. This is hardwired, and if in 500 years English introduces the "complement of whatever",
> well, that would be tk3.»*

The distinction is **structure vs vocabulary**, and it is the one the standing law's test 2 does not
by itself draw. A format is allowed a horizon; designing tkzip so English's argument structure could
change under it buys insurance against an event that ends the project anyway.

**With an obligation attached:** *«a migration because we discover a slot we forgot is not an
option»*. So task 1 is not «pick a good starter set» — it is **enumerate exhaustively and defend the
list as complete**. A miss is a bug, found by the instruments and fixed by redesigning the frame.

### The seam this draws (and it resolves an apparent contradiction)

The Captain ruled in August that closed grammatical classes are **KB rows**. He has now ruled roles
are **code**. Those look opposed; they are not:

- the **slot** — `destination` — is the shape a statement can take → **frame**
- the **words that mark it** — *to · toward · into · onto · as far as* → **`language_closed_classes` rows**

«I walk **to** the station» and «I walk **toward** the station» fill the same box; the marker list
that routes them there is knowledge and grows. Load-bearing at E3.

### The valve that was proposed and refuted

The QM proposed a safety valve: material no box fits gets recorded rather than dropped. The Captain
refuted the slot form of it completely:

> *«"other" doesn't carry ANY meaning, it's not comparable by definition. Sentence-one's "other"
> carrying a "caused by" and sentence-two's "other" carrying a "to whom" — tk2 THINKS they can be
> compared, and they aren't. So then you can propose we add a type in the slot "other"… and in that
> case we just did B, silently moving the slots from frame to knowledge to cover the worst case.
> Which is what we don't want.»*

Correct, and the drift is the whole point: the moment two leftovers need to know whether they are the
same kind of leftover, a role registry has been built by accident, un-curated and unsealed.
**No catch-all slot, ever.**

What survived is a different thing wearing similar clothes: a **diagnostic**, outside the geometry,
never compared, read by the debugging tooling only — because the compiler is the thing that *knows*
it failed to place a word, and throwing that away means re-deriving it later, expensively and
imperfectly. Ruled: *«yes, diagnostic only, outside geometry; we will think about it later, it will
be part of the tooling we build to debug effectively»*.

*(The QM's example for it was badly chosen — «because it was hungry» is a **subordinate clause**, a
second row with a CAUSE operator, not unplaced material. The Captain caught it. The genuinely
unplaceable class is single words inside one clause with no subordinate to hang them on:
«**unfortunately**», «**apparently**», «**twice a week**».)*

### RULING — the schema version

**One integer on every zip: the frame version it was compiled against.** Not because the list is
expected to move, but because E9's translation night must know what it is translating, and it costs

## RULING 2 — the roles are NAMED and VERB-INDEPENDENT

Two philosophies exist and they are incompatible:

- **named** (VerbNet, FrameNet): box 3 is `instrument`, in every sentence, forever
- **numbered** (PropBank): `ARG2` means the recipient for *give*, the substance for *fill*, the
  extent for *rise* — **defined per verb**

> «I gave the book **to Anna**» → ARG2 = recipient
> «I filled the glass **with water**» → ARG2 = substance

Same box, unrelated meanings: compare those two zips and *Anna* is matched against *water* as though
they played the same part. **That is exactly the failure fixed arity exists to prevent**, so the
numbered convention is unusable here — not worse linguistics, just incompatible with comparability.

**Consequence, accepted with the ruling:** this settles open question 2, «suspended» since July.
Named roles force «the fish was eaten by the cat» to compile with *cat* as `agent` — **passive
normalizes to deep roles** rather than following surface order. English word order stops being
information. *(The Captain, unprompted: «it's amazing how the dear old subject and object can split
in different slots now.»)*

## The measurement

Recalling an inventory would not meet a 99.9999% standard, so all three references were read
directly. Corpora fetched into `~/nltk_data` — a **design reference, never a runtime dependency**.

| source | what it gives | measured |
|---|---|---|
| **VerbNet** | named thematic roles over 429 verb classes | **29 distinct roles** |
| **PropBank** | roles annotated on **112,917 real sentences**, with frequencies | 7 core + 13 adjunct labels |
| **FrameNet** | frame elements over 1,221 frames | **981 distinct "core" names**, 532 non-core |

**FrameNet's 981 core names confirm ruling 2 from a third direction**: *Cognizer, Speaker, Victim,
Goods, Protagonist* are frame-specific labels for what VerbNet calls Agent and Theme — the same
disease as `ARG2`. Only FrameNet's **non-core** elements are frame-independent, and those are the
circumstantials: Time 811 · Place 677 · Manner 657 · Degree 457 · Means 437 · Purpose 389 ·
Explanation 380 · Depictive 266 · Duration 263 · Frequency 185 · Result 152 · Instrument 119 · Path 63.

### The finding that mattered most

**PropBank has an "other" box.** `ARGM-ADV`, **10,254 uses — the third most common modifier in the
corpus.** The best-resourced annotation project in English gave in to exactly the temptation the
Captain had just refused.

Opening it and reading the spans dissolved it:

> *«if Congress doesn't act by then» · «Although preliminary findings were reported more than a year
> ago» · «Despite recent declines in yields» · «even though exhaust fans ventilated the area» ·
> «reflecting the country's economic sluggishness» · «probably» · «According to Audit Bureau of
> Circulations» · «without him» · «Along with the note» · «Besides the designer's age»*

Most of it is **subordinate clauses** — conditionals, concessions, participials. PropBank flattens
them into a modifier label because PropBank annotates one verb at a time; a zip has **rows**. What
genuinely survives as candidate boxes is small: epistemic adverbs, evidentials, comitatives,
additives. **The catch-all was never really a bag** — it was a consequence of a flat annotation
scheme, and the Captain's architecture dissolves it.

## RULING 3 — adverbs split four ways; manner is a box; the rotation is one layer up

The Captain's position: *«adverbs qualify the verb, so they are the verb property: action +
property»* — the ad-verb etymology. Right for the largest class, and one class breaks it:

| kind | example | modifies |
|---|---|---|
| **manner** | «I work **relentlessly**» | the verb ✔ his rotation |
| **epistemic / evaluative** | «**Probably** he left» · «**Unfortunately**, he died» | the whole clause |
| **circumstantial** | «**in the kitchen**», «**twice a week**» | fills a box / quantifies |
| **discourse** | «**However**, I work» | the link between rows |

«Unfortunately, he died» does not mean he died in an unfortunate manner — the dying is not being
described at all. **And v1 already agrees**: `modal`, `dubitative`, `ironic` sit beside `negated` as
**clause** fields, never on the verb. PropBank's own labels cut the same four ways (MNR 8,037 ·
ADV 10,254 · TMP 21,169 + LOC 7,410 · DIS 6,097).

*(A fifth, small and nasty: **never · hardly · almost** are structure, not description. «He almost
died» asserts he did **not**. Second standing law: they compile, they get no vector. For the drill.)*

### The Captain's question: how do two sentences sharing a verb stay comparable when adverbs rotate it?

**Averaging fails on the dictionary's own finding.** `(work + relentlessly)/2` versus
`(work + lazily)/2` — but *relentless* and *lazy* are **antonyms**, and antonyms sit **close** in the
topical geometry (love/hate measured **0.86**). The centroid erases the one distinction that mattered.
This is what req 12's *transformed composition, never a centroid* was written against.

**Where the opposite directions come from — and this is what E1 bought.** Two floors: **D** (gloss
overlap) says *relentless ≈ lazy*, same topic, close. **R** (stated relations, **signed**) says
**ANTONYM**, negative. Only R carries a sign, so the displacement direction comes from the stated
half, not the geometric half. Geometry proposes, stated knowledge gates — req 17's *one law at both
floors*, doing real work.

**The property that keeps them comparable:** the transform is **bounded**. The verb may be moved,
never past a floor. `cos(«work relentlessly», «work lazily»)` stays high — both are working — while
the *difference vector* points along a meaningful axis. Same activity, distinguishable execution.

### Where it lives — not a fork, two layers

- **the zip stores the parts** — manner gets a box; nothing lost, the renderer can speak it back
  (req 9), the KB can reason about *relentlessly* as a thing
- **the derived point composes them** — req 12's transformed composition, stored beside the zip and
  epoch-stamped (req 16). **That is where the rotation happens**, and where «I toil» meets «I work
  hard»

Ruled: *«manner as a box, rotation in the derived layer»*. The zip is the skeleton; the derived point
is the thought as one shape.

## GROUP A — the participants

**The free cuts, 29 → 18.** `Patient1/2`, `Actor1/2`, `Theme1/2` are VerbNet notation for symmetric
verbs, not six roles → base role ×1. `Actor` is VerbNet's own hedge → `Agent`. `Proposition` («he
believes **that X**») is a **second row** with an attitude operator, not a box. `Time` and `Extent`
are circumstantial → Group B.

**The five judgment calls — all five ruled as recommended:**

1. **Theme vs Patient → MERGE**, one box `patient`. PropBank merges them (`ARG1`, 106,552 uses — the
   most common label in English); VerbNet splits (Theme 145 / Patient 55). The change-of-state
   distinction is real **in the verb**, not in the participant: *break* already carries «it ends up
   broken». Two boxes would make «I broke it» and «I moved it» incomparable.
2. **Experiencer → KEEP separate from Agent.** «I kicked the dog» (I act) vs «I fear the dog» (it
   happens to me). Collapsing would make «I frighten the dog» and «I fear the dog» one shape with the
   arguments swapped, and they are not — one is an action, one a state. Verbs of feeling and thinking
   are the ones tokeniko will use about **himself** most, which raises the stakes.
3. **Stimulus → MERGE into `patient`.** 4 uses in 429 VerbNet classes; the `experiencer` box already
   marks the sentence as a feeling, so the other filler is unambiguous without its own name.
4. **Recipient · Beneficiary · Destination → ALL THREE.** «gave the book **to Anna**» (she receives)
   · «baked a cake **for Anna**» (she benefits, may never touch it) · «walked **to the station**»
   (a place). Not degrees of one thing: the beneficiary reading **does not entail** Anna got a cake,
   and that entailment difference is what the evaluator reasons with.
5. **The leftovers.** `Material` → `source` · `Product` → `patient` · `Asset` + `Value` + `Extent` →
   one box **`measure`** (money, prices, amounts) · `Attribute`/`Predicate` → one box
   **`complement`** · `Topic` → **kept** («talk **about** X» is a real complement; FrameNet ranks it 67).

**GROUP A — eleven boxes:** `agent` · `patient` · `experiencer` · `recipient` · `beneficiary` ·
`instrument` · `source` · `destination` · `complement` · `topic` · `measure`

## GROUP B — the circumstances

### The six that are boxes

| box | question | example | evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| `location` | where | «I work **in the kitchen**» | LOC 7,410 · Place 677 |
| `time` | when | «I work **on Tuesdays**» | TMP 21,169 · Time 811 |
| `manner` | how | «I work **relentlessly**» | MNR 8,037 · Manner 657 |
| `duration` | how long | «I worked **for two hours**» | Duration 263 |
| `path` | via where | «I walked **through the tunnel**» | Path 63 |
| `comitative` | with whom | «I went **with Anna**» | see below |

**`comitative` is the session's find.** It is **not** in VerbNet's 29 and **not** in PropBank's
adjunct labels. It was found by opening the `ARGM-ADV` bag and reading spans («without him», «along
with the note»). Without it:

> «I ate **with a fork**» → instrument · «I ate **with Anna**» → instrument

Anna becomes a utensil — precisely the silent error the no-valve ruling forbids. *(«I fought with
Anna» stays ambiguous between companion and opponent, and should: the ambiguity is in the English.)*

**And the Captain's own excel draft had it as `with`, before any corpus was consulted.** Two
independent routes to one box is the strongest evidence either of us has that the box is real —
and the reason the origin document now exists (see `origin-the-excel-draft.md`).

### The eight that are NOT boxes — three open questions die here

1. **`purpose` → an operator between rows. (OQ3 ANSWERED.)** «I did it **to save money**» and «I did
   it **for money**» both compile to a second row «[I] save money» joined by PURPOSE. **The payoff:
   the two sentences become the same zip** — which a `purpose` box would have prevented. *Cost, stated
   plainly: the compiler must supply the implicit verb in the PP form. Real work for E3.*
2. **`cause` → the same; and cause-as-participant MERGES into `agent`.** «**The storm** broke the
   window» vs «**John** broke the window»: VerbNet separates them to mark that storms do not intend
   things, but **volition is a property of the storm, readable from the dictionary**. A separate box
   would restate what the filler already says — the same redundancy-that-can-disagree argument that
   kept `sphere` off the heart's level rows.
3. **`depictive` and `result` → second rows. (OQ5 ANSWERED as the ledger leaned.)** «He ate the fish
   **raw**» → «the fish is raw», co-asserted AND. «He painted the door **red**» → «the door is red»,
   joined by **RESULT**. The explicit RESULT operator is what stops «painted the door and then it was
   red» from being derivable as causation — the ledger's condition, met.
4. **`means` → `instrument`, or a row.** «by bus» is a thing; «by selling the car» is an action, and
   actions are already rows. FrameNet's Means/Instrument split (437/119) is really thing-vs-action.
5. **`degree` → rides what it modifies.** «**very** tall» attaches to the complement, «**very**
   slowly» to the manner. Not a part of the event — an intensifier on another part. Composition in
   the derived layer, same as manner.
6. **`frequency` → structure, not a box.** «**always**» is universal quantification over the time
   variable, «**never**» is negation + universal, «**twice a week**» is a count. Quantifiers are
   structure (second standing law) and already need the scope machinery of task 2. *The QM flagged
   this as its least confident recommendation — «twice a week» carries a NUMBER, and if the
   quantifier machinery cannot hold a count, this returns as a box. The Captain ruled it structure.*
7. **modality · negation · epistemic · evaluative → clause properties.** v1 already has `modal`,
   `negated`, `dubitative`, `ironic`. Task 3.
8. **discourse connectives → between-row operators.** DIS is 6,097 uses in PropBank and not one is a
   participant.

**GROUP B — six boxes:** `location` · `time` · `manner` · `duration` · `path` · `comitative`

### RULING 4 — the theatre is DERIVED

The draft encodes «I went from Rome to Genoa» as `[t-2,t-1][r(x),g(x)][r(y),g(y)][r(z),g(z)]` — Rome
and Genoa as the **start and end coordinates of a spacetime interval**, not as fillers of boxes. Read
strictly, that dissolves `location`, `path`, `duration` and most of `time`: Group B would drop from
six boxes to two.

v1 does it the other way: «in the kitchen» is stored as a filler **and** the coordinates are computed
from it (`c_spacetime.py` resolves time, then space, normalizes, emits the 8-float map — the same
width as the draft's four axis pairs).

**The Captain ruled derived**, with the argument that settles it: *«if you think, also Genoa is a
thing you can reason about. It's a city. Somebody is born there. It's not just coordinates.»* The
evaluator must answer «where does he work?» with *the kitchen*, not with a vector. **The theatre is a
numeric projection of the fillers, never a replacement for them.**

### RULING 5 — the noun-phrase record is adopted

The draft's nominal boxes are three fields, not one cell: **`part`** (the quantifier — all / many /
one), **`rel`** (the possessor — «my cat» → `rel=me`), and the noun. Possessors inside indirect boxes
are written inline as `(owner)thing`.

This is literally plan task 1's «the noun-phrase record», which the QM had walked past. It is also
**open question 7's home** (quantity ⊥ determination), and it puts the quantifier **per noun phrase**
rather than v1's single clause-level quantifier read off the subject — «**all** cats eat **some**
fish» needs two, and v1 cannot say it.

### RULING 6 — topicality survives as a non-geometric marker

The draft keeps «the mail was written by John» as `subject=mail`, `agent=John` — **what the sentence
is about AND who did it**. Deep-role normalization (ruling 2) keeps only the second and destroys the
speaker's choice of what to foreground.

Ruled: **normalize the roles so they compare, and carry topicality as one marker outside the
geometry** — «the topic was the patient». Nothing enters the arithmetic; the renderer can say it back
in the voice it was heard in (req 9). *The QM had narrowed something without noticing; the draft is
what caught it.*

### RULING 7 — the complement box, and copular `be` as structure

The draft puts «cute» and «mammal» **directly in the predicate slot**, dropping the copula. The
Captain's own counter-example decided it: *«"the cat became cute" is copular and can't be reduced as
cat cute»*.

Four sentences, one complement, four claims — «is», «became», «seems», «remains». Drop the verb and
three collapse into the first. **But the draft's instinct is right too**, and the second standing law
says why: **plain copular `be` is structure, not content** — it earns no dimension. So:

| | predicate | complement |
|---|---|---|
| «the cat is cute» | — *(copular, compiled)* | cute |
| «the cat became cute» | become | cute |
| «the cat seems cute» | seem | cute |
| «the cat sleeps» | sleep | — |

«The cat is cute» still comes out as **cat + cute, no verb** — the draft exactly — while `become`,
`seem` and `remain` keep a home. *(Exception for the drill: existential `be` — «there **is** a cat» —
is content, not glue.)*
