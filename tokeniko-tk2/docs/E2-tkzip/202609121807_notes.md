**Roughly 60×**, and the cache costs less than a doubling — because E1c's sense vectors turned out to
average **6.78 cells**, not 4,555 floats. The cache is affordable *because of a measurement taken in a
different epic*.

### CASE 3 — why the cache ruling matters more than its size

If the base is rebuilt and the dimensions move, every cached vector's indices become meaningless. That
sounds fatal and is not: **because the KEY is the truth, a rebuild invalidates only the cache.**
Epoch-stamp it and let the sleep phase re-derive lazily — tkzip req 16's pattern, third application.

And even EAGER re-derivation is cheap: ~9 dictionary lookups per sentence, and `space.py` resolves in
microseconds. A 100,000-sentence biography is **seconds**.

> **E9's translation night is for a SCHEMA change. A dictionary rebuild never touches the biography.**

That separation exists *only* because the vector is a cache rather than the stored truth. It is the
strongest consequence of dictionary req 13 and it was not obvious from the requirement's wording.

### CASE 5 — the theatre is stored, but as a cache

The theatre is DERIVED from the fillers (task 1's ruling), but recomputing «yesterday» years later
needs the utterance time (on the document, `Timestamped`) **and** the station's resolution logic,
which is version-dependent. So it is **stored and epoch-stamped**, exactly like the role vectors.
**Truth = the filler + the utterance time.**

### CASE 9 — the constraint tkzip owns, the decision it does not

**Row names are ZIP-LOCAL.** Tseitin names never cross thoughts — a summary references its parents by
**document id**, not by row name. So a zip is self-contained and *can* be embedded in its document.
Whether it *is* embedded is data-modeling's call, and was not taken here.

## CASE 7 — the gap, and the first instance of the Captain's own prediction

Rendering a zip back faithfully (req 9) exposed this:

> «I walk **to** the station» · «I walk **toward** the station»

Both fill `destination`. They do not mean the same thing — *to* implies arrival, *toward* does not.
**If the marker word is not stored, that difference is gone.**

**v1 already stores markers for exactly this reason**, and says so:

> *«the marker LEMMA per marked role — geometry already carries it in the 300 dims; this surfaces it
> out of band so the evaluator can tell «Japan is **IN** Asia» (containment) from «Japan **is** Asia»
> (identity)»*

Two readings were put to the Captain, and they are not exclusive:

- **(α) a seventh field on the nominal record: `marker`** — the preposition actually used. The marker
  words are already `language_closed_classes` rows, so this stores *which row was matched*, not new
  knowledge.
- **(β) the inventory is missing a box** — *toward* may be `direction`, distinct from `destination`.
  PropBank keeps `ARGM-DIR` separate (1,419 uses) and so does FrameNet. If so, this is the first
  instance of the thing the Captain said would happen at task 1: **a missing slot found by an
  instrument, fixed by redesigning the frame** — not by a migration.

**Ruled: α, and «to / toward / into / onto / as far as» goes on the fifty-sentence drill as its own
cluster.** One example is not enough to add a box to a frozen inventory; the drill is the instrument
that should decide (β).
